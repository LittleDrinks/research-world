# Research Plan: Systematic Assessment of Substitutable Container Systems for Cold Food Takeaway Services

## Functional Unit and Study Scope

This research plan defines a rigorous comparative assessment of three genuinely substitutable container systems for **1,000 takeaway cold-food container services** with the following specifications:
- **Volume**: 750 mL serving capacity
- **Temperature range**: 0 to 40°C service conditions
- **Performance requirements**: Leak containment, grease resistance, and food-contact compliance per EU 10/2011 regulation[1]
- **Application**: Single-use or reusable cold food containers (non-carbonated, non-pressurized)

The study explicitly excludes carbonated beverage applications due to fundamentally different pressure and CO₂ barrier requirements that would invalidate functional equivalence across material systems.

## Three Substitutable Systems Under Comparison

### System 1: Recyclable Mono-material rPET Container (Drop-in Circular Route)
- **Material**: Recycled polyethylene terephthalate (rPET) from post-consumer sources
- **End-of-life**: Mechanical recycling in established PET infrastructure
- **Functional equivalence**: Matches performance requirements for cold food applications

### System 2: Certified Compostable Container (Industrial Organics Scenario)
- **Material**: Polylactic acid (PLA) or polyhydroxyalkanoate (PHA) certified to EN 13432 standard[2]
- **End-of-life**: Industrial composting facilities operating at 55–60°C with controlled humidity and microbial activity[2]
- **Functional equivalence**: Validated for cold food applications with appropriate barrier properties

### System 3: Reusable Polypropylene (PP) Container System
- **Material**: Virgin or recycled polypropylene designed for multiple use cycles
- **System**: Includes return logistics, washing infrastructure, and quality control
- **Functional equivalence**: Designed to meet identical performance requirements across reuse cycles

## Methodological Framework (ISO 14040/44 Compatible)

### Goal and Scope Definition
- **Objective**: Compare environmental impacts of three substitutable container systems for cold food takeaway services
- **System boundary**: Cradle-to-grave including raw material extraction, manufacturing, transport, use phase, and end-of-life management
- **Geographic scope**: European Union market with representative infrastructure availability
- **Temporal scope**: 2026 baseline with data quality hierarchy prioritizing recent, region-specific sources

### Life Cycle Inventory (LCI) Data Requirements
All parameters will be treated as measured or sourced inputs requiring empirical collection:

| Parameter Category | Specific Parameters | Data Sources Required |
|-------------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Masses** | Container mass per system (g) | Manufacturer specifications, laboratory measurements |
| **Transport** | Manufacturing-to-distribution distances (km), return logistics distances (km) | Supply chain mapping, GPS tracking data |
| **Reuse System Performance** | Return rate (%), loss rate (%), number of reuse cycles | Operational pilot data, industry benchmarks |
| **Washing** | Energy consumption (kWh/cycle), water consumption (L/cycle), detergent use | Facility monitoring, engineering specifications |
| **End-of-Life Infrastructure** | Collection rates, sorting efficiency, recycling rates, composting facility availability | Eurostat[3], EPA, governmental waste statistics |
| **Material Composition** | Virgin vs. recycled content percentages, additive concentrations | Material safety data sheets, supplier declarations |

### Life Cycle Impact Assessment (LCIA) Methodology
- **Primary method**: ReCiPe 2016 midpoint characterization[4]
- **Impact categories**:
  - Climate change (kg CO₂-eq)
  - Fossil resource scarcity (MJ)
  - Freshwater ecotoxicity (CTUe)
  - Human toxicity (CTUh)
  - Water consumption (m³)
  - Land use (m²·yr)
- **Microplastics consideration**: Treated as evidence gap; recent 2024 research provides preliminary characterization factors for LDPE, PP, and PET in ReCiPe2016[5], but validation against USEtox models required before inclusion

### Data Quality Hierarchy
1. **Primary data**: Direct measurements from operational pilots and laboratory testing
2. **Secondary data**: Peer-reviewed literature, governmental databases (Eurostat, EPA)
3. **Tertiary data**: Industry association reports, standards bodies (CEN, ISO)
4. **Modeled data**: Ecoinvent 3.8 database with regionalization factors
5. **Expert judgment**: Only when no empirical data available, with explicit uncertainty quantification

## Performance Testing Protocol

### Laboratory Performance Tests
- **Leak testing**: ASTM D4169 standard drop tests with water and oil simulants
- **Grease resistance**: Gravimetric analysis of oil absorption after 24-hour exposure
- **Temperature stability**: Dimensional stability testing across 0–40°C range
- **Food contact compliance**: Migration testing per EU 10/2011 requirements[1]

### Infrastructure Feasibility Assessment
- **Recycling infrastructure**: Mapping of rPET collection and reprocessing facilities within target regions
- **Composting infrastructure**: Inventory of EN 13432-certified industrial composting facilities with capacity data
- **Reuse system logistics**: Route optimization modeling for collection and return scenarios

### Operational Return-System Pilot
- **Scale**: 500-container pilot deployment across 3 food service locations
- **Duration**: 12-week operational period
- **Key endpoints**:
  - Actual return rate (%) with confidence intervals
  - Container damage/loss rate (%) by cause category
  - Washing energy and water consumption per cycle
  - Customer acceptance metrics (survey-based)
- **Analysis**: Statistical comparison against industry benchmarks and break-even thresholds

## Reuse Break-even Analysis and Decision Framework

### Transparent Break-even Equation
The environmental break-even point for reusable PP containers versus single-use alternatives is determined by:

```
N_break-even = (E_single - E_production_reusable) / (E_washing - E_end-of-life_single)
```

Where:
- N_break-even = Minimum number of reuse cycles for environmental advantage
- E_single = Environmental impact of single-use container system
- E_production_reusable = Production impact of reusable container
- E_washing = Per-cycle washing impact
- E_end-of-life_single = End-of-life impact of single-use system

### Predeclared Decision Rules
- **Primary decision criterion**: Reusable system demonstrates environmental advantage across ≥3 impact categories at realistic return rates (≥70% based on closed-loop partner data[6])
- **Conditional priority**: Direction 3 (reuse system) becomes working priority only if:
  1. Pilot achieves ≥70% return rate
  2. Break-even cycles ≤20 for climate change impact
  3. Customer acceptance score ≥4.0/5.0
- **Fallback interpretation**: If reuse system fails criteria, recommend optimized single-use system (rPET vs. compostable) based on local infrastructure availability

## Explicit Failure Criteria

### Technical Failures
- **Performance failure**: Any system fails laboratory testing for leak/grease containment
- **Infrastructure failure**: <20% population coverage by appropriate end-of-life infrastructure
- **Economic failure**: Reuse system cost exceeds single-use alternatives by >50% without environmental benefit

### Methodological Failures
- **Data quality failure**: >30% of key parameters rely on expert judgment without uncertainty bounds
- **Functional equivalence failure**: Systems cannot be validated as substitutable for target application
- **Scope creep**: Introduction of non-cold-food applications or pressurized requirements

### Interpretation Failures
- **Overgeneralization**: Extrapolating results beyond defined geographic/temporal scope
- **False equivalence**: Treaking planned work as executed evidence
- **Citation integrity failure**: Using unverifiable or fabricated sources
